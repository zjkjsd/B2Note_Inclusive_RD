    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The combination of \texttt{min\_child\_samples} $=1000$ with only 49 leaves
and 25 boosting rounds gives a deliberately small, heavily regularised model.
That is a reasonable choice for a classifier whose output defines control
regions as well as the signal selection, since a sharply overtrained
discriminant would sculpt those regions differently in data and simulation.

  \label{tab:mva-metrics}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{l c c c c c}
    \hline
    Model & Rounds & \multicolumn{2}{c}{multi-class log-loss} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{\texttt{auc\_mu}} \\
          &        & train & validation & train & validation \\
    \hline
    \texttt{v1} & 50 & 0.771 & 0.894 & 0.923 & 0.909 \\
    \texttt{v2} & 34 & 0.777 & 0.808 & 0.922 & 0.915 \\
    \texttt{v3} & 25 & 0.796 & 0.805 & 0.918 & 0.916 \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The train--validation gap narrows monotonically from \texttt{v1} to
\texttt{v3}: the log-loss gap falls from $0.123$ to $0.009$ and the
\texttt{auc\_mu} gap from $0.014$ to $0.002$.
\texttt{v1} shows the clearest sign of overtraining and \texttt{v3} essentially
none, which is consistent with \texttt{v3} being the model produced by the
current two-stage schedule.
