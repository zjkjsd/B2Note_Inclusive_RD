\subsection{Generic-$B\bar{B}$ composition}
\label{sec:syst:bbbar}
The treatment is to propagate the covariance of the tuned family weights
(Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar}) into the fit as \texttt{normsys} constraints on the
generic-$B\bar{B}$ normalisations, replacing the free normalisation factors
of the current workspace (Sec.~\ref{sec:fit:modifiers}).
Because the family weights are strongly correlated, assigning one independent
constrained parameter per family would discard that correlation.
The intended construction is instead to diagonalise the covariance matrix and
use its eigenvectors as a small set of uncorrelated nuisance parameters, each
varying all five family weights coherently by
$\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\, \hat{v}_{i}$.
\InProgress{this still cannot be applied to the nominal fit.  Two obstacles,
both consequences of the minimum sitting on a parameter bound
(Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar:results}), persist in the regenerated \texttt{run1+run2}
result: for the nominal \texttt{kinematic-2d}, $e$-channel fit, the HESSE and
MINOS uncertainties disagree by up to six orders of magnitude for the two
at-limit parameters (unmeasured 2- and 4-body), while agreeing to within
$\mathcal{O}(10\%)$ for the three parameters that are not pinned; and the
covariance is still near-singular (condition number $\approx 1.9\times10^{6}$,
two eigenvalues below $3\times10^{-7}$), so the eigenvector construction would
still assign essentially no uncertainty to two entire hadronic families.
Enabling MINOS (Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar:results}) has therefore not removed this
obstacle, though it does mean a partial version of this construction ---
restricted to the measured-hadronic, 3-body and 5+-body directions, which are
not pinned in the nominal fit --- may already be usable while the 2-body and
4-body degeneracy remains open.
The method is sound and is implemented in
\texttt{scripts/bbbar\_eigen\_systematics.py}; it requires a fit with an
interior minimum in all five directions.  Whether that is achievable by
relaxing the bounds, requires merging families the tuning region cannot
separate, or reflects a data-preferred weight outside the physical range, is
the three-way question posed in Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar:degeneracy}}

\subsection{Fake-$D$ normalisation}
\label{sec:syst:faked}
Confirmed: the fake-$D$ normalisation is no longer treated as a systematic.
An earlier stage of the analysis considered a candidate $\pm 3.5\%$ prior from
the $D$-mass sideband (recorded in the analysis context document), but the
current, intended treatment is that the $D$-mass sideband channel of the
simultaneous fit (Sec.~\ref{sec:fit:observables}) determines the fake-$D$
normalisation in situ, as a free, unconstrained \texttt{normfactor}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:fit:modifiers}), which is why fake-$D$ does not appear in this
systematics section: fitting it removes the need for an external prior on
it.
