\subsection{Constraint sources and channel independence}
\label{sec:syst:sources}

Per the analyst, each constraint above is intended to come from a dedicated
control sample rather than from an external or literature number
(\texttt{ANALYSIS\_DECISION}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item continuum normalisation (Sec.~\ref{sec:syst:continuum}): the
        $\Upsilon(4S)$ off-resonance sample (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation:regions});
  \item generic-$B\bar{B}$ composition, specifically
        \texttt{bkg\_combinatorial} and \texttt{bkg\_hadronicB\_secondaryL}
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:syst:bbbar}, currently unconstrained in
        Fig.~\ref{fig:pyhf-config}): the BDT-sideband and wrong-charge
        control samples (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation:regions});
  \item fake-$D$ normalisation (Sec.~\ref{sec:syst:faked}): the $D$-mass
        sideband, already fit in situ rather than as an external constraint;
  \item \texttt{bkg\_fakeL} (currently unconstrained in
        Fig.~\ref{fig:pyhf-config}): the collaboration-provided
        \texttt{SystematicFramework} tool~\cite{SystematicFramework};
  \item normalisation-mode ($D\ell\nu$, $D^{*}\ell\nu$) modelling: the
        $q^{2}$ sideband (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation:regions});
  \item $D^{*}\tau\nu$/$D^{**}\tau\nu$ feed-down (already
        \texttt{normsys}-constrained, Sec.~\ref{sec:fit:modifiers}): the
        $D^{*}$-veto sample (Sec.~\ref{sec:recon:dstveto}).
\end{itemize}
\texttt{bkg\_fakeTracks} is not covered by a control-sample plan: per the
analyst, no source to study it has been identified yet.
Sec.~\ref{sec:truth:closure} documents it as excluded from the templates by
\texttt{create\_templates\_new}'s \emph{default} \texttt{sample\_to\_exclude}
argument, but --- confirmed by the analyst --- it \emph{was} included in the
sensitivity workspace, with only a \texttt{staterror} modifier turned on
like \texttt{bkg\_combinatorial}, \texttt{bkg\_fakeL} and
\texttt{bkg\_hadronicB\_secondaryL} (Fig.~\ref{fig:pyhf-config}); the
generator default does not describe this particular workspace.
If no control sample is eventually found for it, the analyst's fallback
plan is to rely on the MC shape as-is and assign a large \texttt{normsys}
of order $\pm 50\%$ rather than leave it fully unconstrained
(\texttt{PLANNED}).

Each of these control samples is reconstructed, and its lepton identified,
separately for the electron and muon channels, so the resulting constraint
is itself channel-specific rather than one external number shared between
the two channels' fits.
This is the missing piece for Sec.~\ref{sec:results}'s combination: the two
channels' \emph{systematic} constraints are independent for the same reason
their data statistics are (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}, Combination paragraph)
--- each is measured from a separate, non-overlapping, lepton-flavour-specific
sample --- so treating the two channels as independent when combining is
justified for the systematic components too, not only the statistical one.

\subsection{Generic-$B\bar{B}$ composition}
\label{sec:syst:bbbar}
The treatment is to propagate the covariance of the tuned family weights
(Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar}) into the fit as \texttt{normsys} constraints on the
generic-$B\bar{B}$ normalisations, replacing the free normalisation factors
of the current workspace (Sec.~\ref{sec:fit:modifiers}).
Because the family weights are strongly correlated, assigning one independent
constrained parameter per family would discard that correlation.
The intended construction is instead to diagonalise the covariance matrix and
use its eigenvectors as a small set of uncorrelated nuisance parameters, each
varying all five family weights coherently by
$\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\, \hat{v}_{i}$.
\InProgress{this still cannot be applied to the nominal fit.  Two obstacles,
both consequences of the minimum sitting on a parameter bound
(Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar:results}), persist in the regenerated \texttt{run1+run2}
result: for the nominal \texttt{kinematic-2d}, $e$-channel fit, the HESSE and
MINOS uncertainties disagree by up to six orders of magnitude for the two
at-limit parameters (unmeasured 2- and 4-body), while agreeing to within
$\mathcal{O}(10\%)$ for the three parameters that are not pinned; and the
covariance is still near-singular (condition number $\approx 1.9\times10^{6}$,
two eigenvalues below $3\times10^{-7}$), so the eigenvector construction would
still assign essentially no uncertainty to two entire hadronic families.
Enabling MINOS (Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar:results}) has therefore not removed this
obstacle, though it does mean a partial version of this construction ---
restricted to the measured-hadronic, 3-body and 5+-body directions, which are
not pinned in the nominal fit --- may already be usable while the 2-body and
4-body degeneracy remains open.
The method is sound and is implemented in
\texttt{scripts/bbbar\_eigen\_systematics.py}; it requires a fit with an
interior minimum in all five directions.  Whether that is achievable by
relaxing the bounds, requires merging families the tuning region cannot
separate, or reflects a data-preferred weight outside the physical range, is
the three-way question posed in Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar:degeneracy}}

\subsection{Fake-$D$ normalisation}
\label{sec:syst:faked}
Confirmed: the fake-$D$ normalisation is no longer treated as a systematic.
An earlier stage of the analysis considered a candidate $\pm 3.5\%$ prior from
the $D$-mass sideband (recorded in the analysis context document), but the
current, intended treatment is that the $D$-mass sideband channel of the
simultaneous fit (Sec.~\ref{sec:fit:observables}) determines the fake-$D$
normalisation in situ, as a free, unconstrained \texttt{normfactor}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:fit:modifiers}), which is why fake-$D$ does not appear in this
systematics section: fitting it removes the need for an external prior on
it.

\subsection{$D^{**}\ell\nu$ and gap-mode modelling}
\label{sec:syst:gap}
Per the analyst, this is expected to be one of the potentially large
systematics, precisely because the current treatment is a shape
substitution motivated by a lack of a trusted alternative
(Sec.~\ref{sec:truth:merging}), not a validated model: the gap-mode template
is built from the $D^{**}\ell\nu$ (broad) shape, with the two components'
normalisations summed, rather than from the generic-MC gap-mode models
themselves.
\InProgress{test alternative models for the gap-mode shape/normalisation
against the current $D^{**}\ell\nu$ (broad)-substitution default, and
quantify the resulting change in the fitted $B\to D\tau\nu$ normalisation;
this is in addition to, and motivated by the same substitution as,
Sec.~\ref{sec:truth:merging}'s existing item on what is lost by the
narrow/broad merge}

As external context only (not an input to this analysis, per the
organisational-reference convention of AGENTS.md): the physics motivation
for gap-mode categories at all is the well-known mismatch between the
inclusive $B\to X_{c}\ell\nu$ branching fraction and the sum of measured
exclusive channels, for which non-resonant $B\to D^{(*)}\pi\pi\ell\nu$ and
$B\to D^{(*)}\eta\ell\nu$ decays were proposed as a fill
\cite{Bernlochner:2012bt}.
The four sibling Belle~II semileptonic-tag notes examined treat this
differently enough that no single precedent is obviously the right one to
follow here, which if anything supports treating the current substitution as
provisional:
\begin{itemize}
  \item one uses dedicated gap-mode signal MC generated via an intermediate
        broad $D^{**}$ resonance (the same substitution used here, but from
        purpose-built MC rather than a post-hoc template swap), assigns a
        $100\%$ uncertainty to the combined gap-mode branching fraction
        (varying all of them together, ``since their true composition is
        uncertain''), and separately varies the broad-$D^{**}$ form-factor
        parameters for the gap-mode decays;
  \item a second instead models the gap as dominated by
        $B\to D^{(*)}\eta\ell\nu$ rather than $D^{(*)}\pi\pi\ell\nu$, and
        assigns separate large uncertainties to the two hypotheses;
  \item a third takes light-lepton gap-mode branching fractions from HFLAV
        averages and derives semitauonic gap-mode branching fractions (not
        available from HFLAV) from predicted $R(H_{c})$ ratios
        \cite{Bernlochner:2023eqn};
  \item a fourth treats gap modes as ordinary $B\to D^{**}\ell\nu$ decays
        for form-factor reweighting purposes (the same choice made here),
        and separately notes that some phase-space-generated non-resonant
        modes in generic MC are removed because their measured branching
        fractions are already saturated by resonant $D^{**}$ production.
\end{itemize}
