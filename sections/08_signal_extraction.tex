\section{Signal Extraction}
\label{sec:fit}

\subsection{Fit observables and channels}
\label{sec:fit:observables}

Yields are extracted from a two-dimensional binned likelihood fit in
$\mmiss$ (\texttt{B0\_recMissM2}) and $\pdl$ (\texttt{p\_D\_l}), performed
simultaneously in two channels:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the $D$-mass signal region, $1.855 < m(K\pi\pi) < 1.885$;
  \item the $D$-mass sideband, $[1.79,1.82] \cup [1.92,1.95]$
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:recon:d}), which constrains the fake-$D$ background.
\end{itemize}
These two are the only channels in the fit.
The $q^{2}$, $D^{*}$-veto, off-resonance, BDT-sideband and wrong-charge
regions of Sec.~\ref{sec:validation} are cross-checks and do not enter the
likelihood.

The templates are built from the same MC categories in both channels, so the
sideband determines the fake-$D$ normalisation and shape in situ rather than
through an external prior.

Three properties of the generated workspace require attention.

\paragraph{Generic-$B\bar{B}$ normalisations.}
The generic-$B\bar{B}$ families currently enter as free normalisation
factors.
That is appropriate in the control-region context the workspace generator was
originally written for, but not in the signal-region fit: floating them there
discards the constraint obtained by the tuning of Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar} and
adds five free parameters that the signal normalisation must be separated
from.
The intended configuration is instead a \texttt{normsys} constraint per
family, with the width taken from the uncertainty of the tuned weights
(\texttt{ANALYSIS\_DECISION}).

MINOS enabled (Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar:results}), the nominal \texttt{kinematic-2d}
tuning gives a genuine, non-boundary MINOS interval for the measured-hadronic,

It is not yet appropriate for the 2-body and 4-body weights: both remain

\paragraph{Parameter bounds.}
Every normalisation parameter is generated with bounds $[-5, 5]$, which
permits negative values.
This is deliberate (\texttt{ANALYSIS\_DECISION}).
The intent is not that a negative yield is physical, but that the minimiser
should be free to traverse the negative region so that the reported minimum is
the global one, rather than a value pushed against a boundary at zero.
A parameter constrained at zero cannot be distinguished from a parameter whose
true minimum lies below it, and a best-fit value that sits on a physical
boundary carries no meaningful parabolic uncertainty --- the same pathology
documented for the generic-$B\bar{B}$ weights in
Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar:degeneracy}.
Allowing the fit to explore negative values and observing that the minimum
lands in the positive region is therefore evidence, whereas forcing
positivity and observing a positive result is not.

The freedom is intended for the free \texttt{normfactor} parameters only.
Constrained parameters are not affected: a \texttt{normsys} modifier is
parameterised by a nuisance parameter with its own Gaussian constraint and an
interpolated response, so the $[-5,5]$ range does not apply to it in the same
way.

\paragraph{MC statistical uncertainty.}
All samples in a channel share a single \texttt{staterror} modifier name,
\texttt{mcStat\_ch$n$}, so the MC statistical uncertainty is treated as one
constrained nuisance parameter per channel per bin, summing the samples'
individual MC statistical uncertainties in quadrature into that one
parameter, rather than as an independent nuisance parameter per sample per
bin.
This is the ``lite'' Beeston--Barlow treatment.
An earlier version of this note justified it by treating the per-bin MC
statistical fluctuations of different truth categories drawn from one
generic-MC production as themselves correlated, ``since they trace back to
the same finite set of generated events.''
That justification was incorrect and has been removed
(\texttt{ANALYSIS\_DECISION}, corrected): the categories are mutually
exclusive subsets of one generated sample, and in the standard binned
template-statistics model each category's per-bin count is an independent
Poisson variate with its own finite-sample uncertainty, exactly as it would
be if the categories came from separate productions.
Sharing one production does not by itself correlate their fluctuations; if
anything, conditioning on a fixed total count in a bin would induce
\emph{anti}-correlation between categories (a multinomial split of a fixed
total), not the positive correlation the earlier text asserted, and no such
conditioning is imposed here.
The real justification for the lite treatment is practical rather than a
consequence of shared origin: every sample's statistics here are large
enough that the lite and full (one nuisance parameter per sample per bin)
treatments are expected to differ only slightly, and the lite treatment
keeps the nuisance-parameter count at (channels $\times$ bins) rather than
(channels $\times$ bins $\times$ samples), which is both simpler and
cheaper to fit.
This is unrelated to whether the samples in a channel come from one MC
production or several: the same lite-versus-full trade-off applies either
way, so a channel mixing generic and signal-enriched MC
(Sec.~\ref{sec:data:mc}) does not need a different number of \texttt{staterror}
parameters than a single-production channel on these grounds.
\InProgress{quantify, rather than assert, that the lite and full treatments
give a small difference here, given the actual per-bin statistics of each

\subsection{Fit validation}
\label{sec:fit:validation}

Sec.~\ref{sec:results}; the method is to fit the Asimov dataset (the exact
sum of all template samples) once per channel with every \emph{constrained}

width consistent with $1$ is the pass criterion.  A linearity check repeats
this at several injected normalisation values spanning a wide range (for
example corresponding to $R(D)$ from about half to double its expected
value) and fits the mean fitted value against the injected value, requiring
close to unit slope and zero intercept.  Per-nuisance-parameter pull plots
and a full correlation matrix are produced alongside, and a
tornado/ranking plot of each nuisance parameter's impact on the parameter of
interest is a useful addition once systematics enter the fit
(Sec.~\ref{sec:syst})}
