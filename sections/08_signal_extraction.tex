\section{Signal Extraction}
\label{sec:fit}

\subsection{Fit observables and channels}
\label{sec:fit:observables}

Yields are extracted from a two-dimensional binned likelihood fit in
$\mmiss$ (\texttt{B0\_recMissM2}) and $\pdl$ (\texttt{p\_D\_l}), performed
simultaneously in two channels:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the $D$-mass signal region, $1.855 < m(K\pi\pi) < 1.885$;
  \item the $D$-mass sideband, $[1.79,1.82] \cup [1.92,1.95]$
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:recon:d}), which constrains the fake-$D$ background.
\end{itemize}
These two are the only channels in the fit.
The $q^{2}$, $D^{*}$-veto, off-resonance, BDT-sideband and wrong-charge
regions of Sec.~\ref{sec:validation} are cross-checks and do not enter the
likelihood.

The templates are built from the same MC categories in both channels, so the
sideband determines the fake-$D$ normalisation and shape in situ rather than
through an external prior.

\subsection{Templates}
\label{sec:fit:templates}

Templates are filled per truth category with per-bin statistical
uncertainties from the sum of squared weights, then trimmed to bins whose
summed content passes the population threshold and flattened.
Three categories are excluded before filling
(Sec.~\ref{sec:truth:closure}), and the narrow and broad $D^{**}\ell\nu$
categories are merged (Sec.~\ref{sec:truth:merging}).

\subsection{Parameter of interest}
\label{sec:fit:poi}

The parameter of interest in the generated workspace is the normalisation of
the $B \to D \tau \nu$ template, \texttt{\$D$\backslash$tau$\backslash$nu\$\_norm}.
$R(D)$ is therefore not fitted directly; it is derived from the fitted
signal and normalisation yields.

Three properties of the generated workspace require attention.

\paragraph{Generic-$B\bar{B}$ normalisations.}
The generic-$B\bar{B}$ families currently enter as free normalisation
factors.
That is appropriate in the control-region context the workspace generator was
originally written for, but not in the signal-region fit: floating them there
discards the constraint obtained by the tuning of Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar} and
adds five free parameters that the signal normalisation must be separated
from.
The intended configuration is instead a \texttt{normsys} constraint per
family, with the width taken from the uncertainty of the tuned weights
(\texttt{ANALYSIS\_DECISION}).
\InProgress{no usable width exists yet.  Every stored tuning result sits on a
parameter bound (Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar:degeneracy}), so its uncertainties
cannot be propagated.  This constraint can only be set once a tuning fit
reaches an interior minimum}

\paragraph{Parameter bounds.}
Every normalisation parameter is generated with bounds $[-5, 5]$, which
permits negative values.
This is deliberate (\texttt{ANALYSIS\_DECISION}).
The intent is not that a negative yield is physical, but that the minimiser
should be free to traverse the negative region so that the reported minimum is
the global one, rather than a value pushed against a boundary at zero.
A parameter constrained at zero cannot be distinguished from a parameter whose
true minimum lies below it, and a best-fit value that sits on a physical
boundary carries no meaningful parabolic uncertainty --- the same pathology
documented for the generic-$B\bar{B}$ weights in
Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar:degeneracy}.
Allowing the fit to explore negative values and observing that the minimum
lands in the positive region is therefore evidence, whereas forcing
positivity and observing a positive result is not.

The freedom is intended for the free \texttt{normfactor} parameters only.
Constrained parameters are not affected: a \texttt{normsys} modifier is
parameterised by a nuisance parameter with its own Gaussian constraint and an
interpolated response, so the $[-5,5]$ range does not apply to it in the same
way.
\AnalysisTBD{once the generic-$B\bar{B}$ families move from
\texttt{normfactor} to \texttt{normsys} (above), confirm that no constrained
component is left with an unconstrained $[-5,5]$ normalisation, and state
what is done if a free normalisation returns a negative best-fit value ---
whether the result is reported as-is, or the fit is repeated with a
positivity requirement as a cross-check}

\paragraph{MC statistical uncertainty.}
All samples in a channel share a single \texttt{staterror} modifier name,
\texttt{mcStat\_ch$n$}, so the MC statistical uncertainty is treated as one
parameter per channel correlated across samples rather than as a per-sample
uncertainty.
\AnalysisTBD{confirm that this is the intended treatment, and state its
effect relative to independent per-sample statistical uncertainties}
