\section{Signal Extraction}
\label{sec:fit}

\subsection{Fit observables, fitting region, and PYHF binning}
\label{sec:fit:observables}

Yields are extracted from a two-dimensional binned likelihood fit in
$\mmiss$ (\texttt{B0\_recMissM2}) and $\pdl$ (\texttt{p\_D\_l}), performed
simultaneously in two channels:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the $D$-mass signal region, $1.855 < m(K\pi\pi) < 1.885$;
  \item the $D$-mass sideband, $[1.79,1.82] \cup [1.92,1.95]$
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:recon:d}), which constrains the fake-$D$ background.
\end{itemize}
These two are the only channels in the fit.
The $q^{2}$, $D^{*}$-veto, off-resonance, BDT-sideband and wrong-charge
regions of Sec.~\ref{sec:validation} are cross-checks and do not enter the
likelihood.

The templates are built from the same MC categories in both channels.

 We start with fine binning in the two variables, which is defined by a
 1-D list of bin edges, a, for MM$^2$ and list of bin edges, b, for $|p_D|+ |p_\ell|$. Thus lists are used to form a two-dimensional histogram, which contains about $2000$ bins. Templates from MC
have about the equivalent of four times data luminosity. We require at least 10 counts in each bin of the template. Otherwise the bin is removed from the template. The resulting two-dimensional (2D) histogram is flattened into a one
dimensional (1D) histogram, which is used for the final PYHF fit.

For data, we will use the same bins as in the MC templates.

\subsection{Parameter of interest}
\label{sec:fit:poi}

The parameter of interest is the normalization of
the $B \to D \tau \nu$ template, \texttt{\$D$\backslash$tau$\backslash$nu\$\_norm}.
At the moment, $R(D)$ is therefore not fitted directly; it is derived from the fitted
signal and normalization yields.

\paragraph{Parameter bounds.}
Every normalization parameter is generated with bounds $[-5, 5]$, which
permits negative values. Constrained parameters are not affected: a \texttt{normsys} modifier is
parameterized by a nuisance parameter with its own Gaussian constraint and an
interpolated response, so the $[-5,5]$ range does not apply to it in the same
way.

\paragraph{MC statistical uncertainty.}
All samples in a channel share a single \texttt{staterror} modifier name,
\texttt{mcStat\_ch$n$}, so the MC statistical uncertainty is treated as one
constrained nuisance parameter per channel per bin, summing the samples'
individual MC statistical uncertainties in quadrature into that one
parameter, rather than as an independent nuisance parameter per sample. 
This is the ``lite'' Beeston--Barlow treatment (see Computer Physics Communications, volume 77, issue 2, pages 219–228).
We note that the Belle II semileptonic $R(D^{(*)})$ paper used the same treatment.




\subsection{Fit validation}
\label{sec:fit:validation}

We determine the $R(D)$ sensitivity using an Asimov fit.
The results are given in Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. 
The method is to fit the Asimov dataset (the exact
sum of all template samples) once per channel with every \emph{constrained}
modifier (\texttt{normsys}, \texttt{staterror}) fixed while the free
\texttt{normfactor} normalizations ($D\ell\nu$, $D^{*}\ell\nu$,
$D^{**}\ell\nu$~+~gap, \texttt{bkg\_fakeD}; Table~\ref{tab:fit-parameters})
remain floating alongside the parameter of interest, which gives the
uncertainty from data statistics alone. We then re-fit repeatedly with one further
constrained modifier freed at a time, and attribute each one's systematic
contribution to the quadrature difference between the new and previous
returned uncertainty on the $B\to D\tau\nu$ normalization


Toy studies, pull distributions and linearity tests are not yet done.
The machinery exists in \texttt{utilities.pyhf\_utils} and
\texttt{utilities.toy\_utils} and in \texttt{Fit\_toys/FitToys\_b2luigi.py};
producing and recording the nominal study is planned but not yet done.
