\section{Multivariate Background Suppression}
\label{sec:mva}

A four-class LightGBM classifier separates correctly reconstructed signal-like
candidates from the three dominant background topologies.
Its four output probabilities --- \texttt{sig\_prob}, \texttt{fakeD\_prob},
\texttt{continuum\_prob} and \texttt{combinatorial\_prob} --- define both the
final event selection and several of the control regions of
Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}.

\subsection{Target classes}
\label{sec:mva:classes}

The four classes correspond to the truth categories of
Sec.~\ref{sec:truth}: correctly reconstructed signal-like candidates,
fake-$D$ candidates, continuum events, and combinatorial candidates in which
the $D$ and the lepton come from different $B$ mesons.
Choosing these four rather than a single signal-versus-background
discriminant is what allows the same classifier to define the fake-$D$ and
combinatorial-enriched control regions used later.

\subsection{Training configuration}
\label{sec:mva:training}

Training uses \texttt{4\_LightGBM\_Multiclass\_Training.py} with a gradient
boosted decision tree (\texttt{gbdt}) and the \texttt{multiclass} objective
over four classes, optimising multi-class log-loss and monitoring
\texttt{auc\_mu}.
The sample is split 80/20 into training and validation subsets with a fixed
random seed, and per-event training weights are applied.
The training and validation subsets are disjoint by construction, being
complementary subsets of the same frame.
\AnalysisTBD{demonstrate that the sample used to train the classifier does not
overlap with the sample used to build the fit templates.  A disjoint
train/validation split within one frame does not by itself establish
independence from the templates}

The hyper-parameters are hard-coded in the training script rather than taken
from a tuning study.
The separate tuner, \texttt{2\_LightGBM\_Tuner.py}, runs an Optuna study for
the \emph{binary} off-resonance data/MC check of
\texttt{3\_LightGBM\_Binary\_Training.py}; its study is not consumed by the
four-class training, and its default \texttt{multiclass} objective has no
matching \texttt{num\_class} configuration.
It therefore does not tune the final classifier in its current form.

\subsection{Input variables}
\label{sec:mva:inputs}

The training variables are defined in
\texttt{utilities.training\_variables} and fall into three groups:
continuum-suppression variables (a subset of the KSFW moments and
\texttt{cosTBTO}), $D$-vertex and Dalitz variables
(\texttt{D\_A1FflightDistanceSig\_IP} and the two two-body invariant-mass
combinations of the $D$ daughters), and $B$-level variables
(\texttt{B0\_CMS\_cos\_angle\_0\_1}, \texttt{B0\_D\_l\_DisSig},
$M_{\mathrm{bc}}^{\mathrm{ROE}}$ and $\Delta E^{\mathrm{ROE}}$).

Variables are excluded on two distinct grounds, both recorded as comments in
the source, and the distinction matters:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Correlation with the fit observables}:
        \texttt{thrustBm}, \texttt{KSFWV1}, \texttt{KSFWV2},
        \texttt{KSFWV11} and \texttt{KSFWV12} are excluded because they
        correlate with $\mmiss$ or $\pdl$.
        Including them would let the selection sculpt the very distributions
        the fit uses, biasing the extracted yields.
  \item \textbf{Data/MC mismodelling}: \texttt{R2}, \texttt{thrustOm},
        \texttt{cosTBz}, \texttt{KSFWV8}, \texttt{KSFWV14}--\texttt{KSFWV18},
        \texttt{D\_vtxReChi2}, \texttt{B0\_vtxReChi2} and
        \texttt{B0\_TagVReChi2IP} are excluded because they are known to be
        mismodelled.
        A classifier trained on a mismodelled variable transfers that
        mismodelling into the selection efficiency.
\end{itemize}
The commented-out variable lists retained in \texttt{utilities.py} correspond
to earlier configurations that included these variables; they are superseded
and are not the nominal input set.

\subsection{Working point}
\label{sec:mva:wp}

The nominal signal selection is \texttt{lgb\_tight}:
\begin{equation}
  \texttt{sig\_prob} > 0.5,\quad
  \texttt{fakeD\_prob} < 0.06,\quad
  \texttt{continuum\_prob} < 0.4,\quad
  \texttt{combinatorial\_prob} < 0.6 .
  \label{eq:lgb-tight}
\end{equation}
It was optimised using a figure of merit.
Two further selections exist and are not the nominal signal selection:
\texttt{lgb\_loose}, used for preliminary studies and as a file-size filter
and chosen by inspection; and \texttt{lgb\_comb}, which selects a
combinatorial-enriched region.
