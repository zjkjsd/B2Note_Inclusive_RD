\section{Multivariate Background Suppression}
\label{sec:mva}

A four-class LightGBM classifier separates correctly reconstructed signal-like
candidates from the three dominant background topologies.
Its four output probabilities --- \texttt{sig\_prob}, \texttt{fakeD\_prob},
\texttt{continuum\_prob} and \texttt{combinatorial\_prob} --- define both the
final event selection and several of the control regions of
Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}.

\subsection{Target classes}
\label{sec:mva:classes}

The four training targets are assigned in
\texttt{Notebooks/2\_create\_BDTtrainingSet.ipynb} as
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c l l}
  % narrow content; kept simple
  \hline
  Target & Class & Output probability \\
  \hline
  0 & correctly reconstructed signal-like & \texttt{sig\_prob} \\
  1 & fake $D$ & \texttt{fakeD\_prob} \\
  2 & continuum & \texttt{continuum\_prob} \\
  3 & combinatorial & \texttt{combinatorial\_prob} \\
  \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
and correspond to the truth categories of Sec.~\ref{sec:truth}.
Choosing four classes rather than a single signal-versus-background
discriminant is what allows one classifier to serve three purposes: the signal
selection of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lgb-tight}, the fake-$D$ suppression that defines
the generic-$B\bar{B}$ tuning region (Eq.~\eqref{eq:bbbar-region}), and the
combinatorial-enriched control region of Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}.
A binary classifier would have required a separate training for each.

\PreliminaryResult{each of the four classes is subsampled to exactly
$500\,000$ events (\texttt{pandas.DataFrame.sample(n=500000,
random\_state=0)}) before training, from the reconstructed class sizes
quoted in Sec.~\ref{sec:truth:composition}; \texttt{training\_weight} is set
to $1$ for every class and every event.
This is a change from an earlier configuration, in which the full,
unequal-sized class samples were used together with a per-class
\texttt{training\_weight} chosen so that (sample size) $\times$
(\texttt{training\_weight}) was constant across classes; truncating every
class to the same size removed the need for that weighting.}

\subsection{Training configuration}
\label{sec:mva:training}

Training uses \texttt{4\_LightGBM\_Multiclass\_Training.py} with gradient
boosted decision trees (\texttt{gbdt}) and the \texttt{multiclass} objective
over four classes, optimising multi-class log-loss and monitoring
\texttt{auc\_mu}.
The sample is split 80/20 into training and validation subsets with a fixed
random seed, and per-event training weights are applied.
The two subsets are disjoint by construction, being complementary subsets of
the same frame.

Training proceeds in two stages: 15 boosting rounds at a fixed learning rate
of $0.5$, followed by 10 further rounds in which the learning rate decays as
$0.5 \times 0.99^{i}$, with early stopping after 3 and 2 rounds without
improvement respectively.
The large initial learning rate with few rounds, followed by a decaying tail,
trades some final accuracy for a very small ensemble.
The nominal model carries 100 trees, four per boosting iteration.
The full hyper-parameter set is given in Table~\ref{tab:mva-hyperparameters}.

The training phase space can be read back from the stored model: the recorded
feature ranges show $M_{\mathrm{bc}}^{\mathrm{ROE}} > 5$~GeV$/c^{2}$ and
$-4 < \Delta E^{\mathrm{ROE}} < 1$~GeV, so the classifier was trained after
the offline requirements of Eq.~\eqref{eq:offline-cut} and is only defined
within them.

The hyper-parameters are hard-coded in the training script rather than taken
from a tuning study.
The separate tuner, \texttt{2\_LightGBM\_Tuner.py}, runs an Optuna study for
the \emph{binary} off-resonance data/MC check of
\texttt{3\_LightGBM\_Binary\_Training.py}; its study is not consumed by the
four-class training, and its default \texttt{multiclass} objective has no
matching \texttt{num\_class} configuration.
It therefore does not tune the final classifier in its current form.

Three trained models are stored, \texttt{lgbm\_multiclass\_v1} through
\texttt{v3}, with 50, 34 and 25 recorded boosting rounds; only \texttt{v3}
matches the two-stage schedule above.
\texttt{v3} is the nominal, up-to-date model; \texttt{v1} and \texttt{v2} are
superseded and are kept only as history at the pinned commit, to be removed
in a future update (\texttt{ANALYSIS\_DECISION}).
The selection working point of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lgb-tight} refers to
\texttt{v3}.

\subsection{Input variables}
\label{sec:mva:inputs}

The training variables are defined in
\texttt{utilities.training\_variables} and fall into three groups:
continuum-suppression variables (a subset of the KSFW moments and
\texttt{cosTBTO}), $D$-vertex and Dalitz variables
(\texttt{D\_A1FflightDistanceSig\_IP} and the two two-body invariant-mass
combinations of the $D$ daughters), and $B$-level variables
(\texttt{B0\_CMS\_cos\_angle\_0\_1}, \texttt{B0\_D\_l\_DisSig},
$M_{\mathrm{bc}}^{\mathrm{ROE}}$ and $\Delta E^{\mathrm{ROE}}$).

Variables are excluded on two distinct grounds, both recorded as comments in
the source, and the distinction matters:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Correlation with the fit observables}:
        \texttt{thrustBm}, \texttt{KSFWV1}, \texttt{KSFWV2},
        \texttt{KSFWV11} and \texttt{KSFWV12} are excluded because they
        correlate with $\mmiss$ or $\pdl$.
        Including them would let the selection sculpt the very distributions
        the fit uses, biasing the extracted yields.
  \item \textbf{Data/MC mismodelling}: \texttt{R2}, \texttt{thrustOm},
        \texttt{cosTBz}, \texttt{KSFWV8}, \texttt{KSFWV14}--\texttt{KSFWV18},
        \texttt{D\_vtxReChi2}, \texttt{B0\_vtxReChi2} and
        \texttt{B0\_TagVReChi2IP} are excluded because they are known to be
        mismodelled.
        A classifier trained on a mismodelled variable transfers that
        mismodelling into the selection efficiency.
\end{itemize}
The commented-out variable lists retained in \texttt{utilities.py} correspond
to earlier configurations that included these variables; they are superseded
and are not the nominal input set.

\subsection{Working point}
\label{sec:mva:wp}

The nominal signal selection is \texttt{lgb\_tight}:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    &\texttt{sig\_prob} > 0.5, \qquad
     \texttt{fakeD\_prob} < 0.06, \\
    &\texttt{continuum\_prob} < 0.4, \qquad
     \texttt{combinatorial\_prob} < 0.6 .
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:lgb-tight}
\end{equation}
It was optimised using a figure of merit.
Two further selections exist and are not the nominal signal selection.
\texttt{lgb\_loose} is used for preliminary studies and as a file-size filter,
and was chosen by inspection.
\texttt{lgb\_comb} defines the combinatorial-enriched region, in which the
generic-$B\bar{B}$ data/simulation comparisons of Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}
are made.
