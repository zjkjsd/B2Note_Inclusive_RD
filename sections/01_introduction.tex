\subsection{Physics motivation}
\label{sec:intro:motivation}

The ratios
\begin{equation}
  R(D^{(*)}) \equiv
  \frac{\mathcal{B}(B \to D^{(*)}\tau\bar\nu_{\tau})}
       {\mathcal{B}(B \to D^{(*)}\ell\bar\nu_{\ell})},
  \qquad \ell = e, \mu ,
\end{equation}
are direct tests of lepton flavour universality in the Standard Model (SM):
the $B \to D^{(*)}\ell\bar\nu_\ell$ and $B \to D^{(*)}\tau\bar\nu_\tau$ decay
rates differ, at tree level, only through the charged-lepton mass and phase
space, so that $R(D^{(*)})$ is predicted with small theoretical uncertainty
and is largely free of the hadronic form-factor uncertainties that limit the
individual branching fractions.
A measured value in excess of the SM prediction would point to new physics
coupling preferentially to the third-generation lepton, such as a charged
Higgs boson or a leptoquark~\cite{Fajfer:2012vx,Tanaka:2012nw}.
Because a charged-Higgs contribution to $B \to D^{*}\tau\bar\nu_\tau$ is
suppressed by the $D^{*}$ momentum in a way that a charged-Higgs
contribution to $B \to D\tau\bar\nu_\tau$ is not, $R(D)$ is more sensitive
than $R(D^{*})$ to this class of scalar couplings~\cite{Fajfer:2012vx,
Tanaka:2012nw}, which is part of the motivation for measuring $R(D)$ on its
own rather than only in combination with $R(D^{*})$.

Figures~\ref{fig:rd-uncertainties} and~\ref{fig:rd-rdst-prelim} summarise the
current experimental and theoretical status.
The first round of $R(D)$ measurements at Belle and Belle~II used the Full
Event Interpretation (FEI)~\cite{Keck:2018lcd} to reconstruct the companion
$B$ meson exclusively, in either a hadronic or a semileptonic decay mode (for
example Ref.~\cite{Belle:2019rba}).
Reconstructing the rest of the event (ROE) inclusively instead of tagging it
exclusively is expected to recover roughly an order of magnitude more signal
events, at the cost of a much larger generic-$B\bar{B}$ background
(Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar}) that the tag-side reconstruction would otherwise
reject.
Because the two approaches select statistically independent subsets of the
same data --- an event either has a companion $B$ that the FEI reconstructs
or it does not --- an inclusive-tagging measurement provides information
independent of the existing FEI-based results and a cross-check of them,
rather than a refinement of the same event sample.

For an inclusively-tagged analysis, the $B^{0} \to D^{-}\ell^{+}$ mode (as
opposed to $B^{+} \to \bar D^{0}\ell^{+}$ or a combination of the two) is used
because of its small $D^{*}$ feed-down and the comparatively long $D^{+}$
lifetime, both of which keep the $D$-mass reconstruction and the $D^{*}$ veto
of Sec.~\ref{sec:recon:dstveto} clean.

Only the $D^{-}$ and $\ell^{+}$ are explicitly reconstructed on the signal
side (Sec.~\ref{sec:reconstruction}); everything else is assigned to the ROE.
The signal decay has three missing neutrinos, and the companion $B$ is
reconstructed inclusively, so neither $B$ meson's kinematics is tightly
constrained.
This has two consequences for the analysis strategy.
First, because so little kinematic information is available at
reconstruction level, a selection based on a handful of variables cannot
reduce the background enough on its own, and both signal and background
retain very large yields through reconstruction.
Second, because the phase space is only loosely constrained, signal and
background events populate similar regions of the available kinematic
variables, so that the shapes of candidate fitting variables are similar
between signal and background.
Together these two consequences make an effective, high-dimensional
background suppression necessary, which is why a multi-class BDT
(Sec.~\ref{sec:mva}) rather than a small set of selection cuts is used.
After the BDT selection, yields for the signal and normalisation modes are
extracted from a two-dimensional binned likelihood fit in the missing mass
squared $\mmiss$ and $\pdl$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:fit}).
As anticipated from the loosely constrained phase space, many components
retain a similar shape in this plane even after the BDT selection, and their
distributions are broad and overlapping, which is what makes the
two-dimensional yield extraction difficult; the correlation between the BDT
training variables and the two fit variables is checked to be small
(Appendix~\ref{app:mva:todo}), so the BDT selection is not expected to be the
cause of that overlap.

\subsection{Analysis signature}
\label{sec:intro:signature}
The signal-side $B$ is reconstructed as $B^{0} \to D^{-} \ell^{+}$ with
$D^{-} \to K^{+} \pi^{-} \pi^{-}$; the tag-side $B$ is represented by the
rest of the event rather than by an exclusive tag reconstruction
(Sec.~\ref{sec:reconstruction}).
Signal and normalisation yields are extracted from a two-dimensional binned
likelihood fit in $\mmiss$ and $\pdl$, performed simultaneously in a
$D$-mass signal region and a $D$-mass sideband (Sec.~\ref{sec:fit}).
The dominant experimental challenge is the generic-$B\bar{B}$ background,
which is treated in Sec.~\ref{sec:bbbar}.

Two features of the ordering are worth stating explicitly, because they
constrain what can be changed independently.
The classifier is trained after the offline selection, so its outputs are
defined only inside that selection and a change to the offline cut invalidates
the training.
And the generic-$B\bar{B}$ reweighting is derived in a region defined by the
classifier output, so it depends on the classifier in a way the
collaboration-recommended corrections do not: retraining the classifier
requires re-deriving those weights.
