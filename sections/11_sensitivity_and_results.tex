\section{Expected Sensitivity and Results}
\label{sec:results}

No data result for the signal region is shown while the analysis is blinded.
This section instead reports an Asimov (data-equals-expectation) sensitivity
study: the fitted data is the exact sum of all template samples, so the
fitted normalisations are expected to return $1$ and constrained nuisance
parameters $0$: the only meaningful output is the returned \emph{uncertainty}
on the parameter of interest, the $B\to D\tau\nu$ normalisation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:fit:poi}), which is what this section calls the sensitivity.
The method is described in Sec.~\ref{sec:fit:validation}: fit with every
\emph{constrained} modifier fixed while the free \texttt{normfactor}
normalisations ($D\ell\nu$, $D^{*}\ell\nu$, $D^{**}\ell\nu$~+~gap,
\texttt{bkg\_fakeD}) remain floating, to obtain the data-statistics-only
uncertainty, then free one further constrained modifier at a time and
attribute its systematic contribution to the quadrature difference between
the new and previous returned uncertainty.
Electron and muon channels are fit separately, each against its own
two-channel (signal region plus sideband) workspace, and the two resulting
uncertainties are combined at the end (below) rather than fit jointly.

The $\gtrsim$ notation reflects the outlook, not the quadrature gap noted in
the caption above: Sec.~\ref{sec:syst} lists several identified systematics
($D^{**}\ell\nu$/gap-mode modelling, form factors, tracking and PID
efficiency, fit bias) not yet enabled as modifiers in this workspace, so the
totals above are expected to grow further, not shrink, as the fit model is
completed.
Fake-$D$ is deliberately absent from that list: Sec.~\ref{sec:syst:faked}
confirms it is no longer treated as an external systematic at all, since the
sideband channel already fits it in situ.

\paragraph{Combination.}
The electron channel is expected to be, and is, more sensitive than the muon
channel: the tighter muon momentum requirement ($p_{\mu} > 0.6$\,GeV$/c$
versus $p_{e} > 0.3$\,GeV$/c$, Sec.~\ref{sec:reconstruction}) removes a larger
fraction of $B\to D\tau\nu$ signal because the signal lepton momentum
spectrum peaks at low momentum, giving the muon channel roughly half the
signal-enriched $D\tau\nu$ statistics of the electron channel
(Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity-sample-sizes}).
The two channels' uncertainties are combined as an inverse-variance-weighted
average, $\sigma_{\rm comb} = \left(\sigma_{e}^{-2} +
\sigma_{\mu}^{-2}\right)^{-1/2}$, which gives $\sigma_{\rm comb} = 12.1\%$
for $14.5\%$ and $22.1\%$ above, close to but not exactly the analyst's
quoted $12.2\%$ (a $0.1$ percentage-point difference); as with the
per-channel totals above, this is treated as part of the preliminary,
not-yet-reconciled numbers rather than corrected to match.
This formula combines two \emph{independent} measurements; whether that
assumption holds was raised in review and answered in two parts.
For the data-statistics component, independence is solid: the $e$ and $\mu$
channels are reconstructed from different, non-overlapping candidates (a
different lepton flavour is required for each), and the rate of a lepton
being misidentified across flavours is small enough that cross-contamination
between the two samples is negligible (\texttt{ANALYSIS\_DECISION}, per the
analyst).
For the systematic components (the $D^{*}\tau\nu$/$D^{**}\tau\nu$ and
continuum \texttt{normsys} constraints), independence was raised in review
as a separate question from data statistics: each channel's workspace
carries its own copy of these nuisance parameters rather than a single
shared one, so the two Asimov fits \emph{as implemented} are formally
independent of each other, but that alone does not establish that treating
them as independent is \emph{physically} correct --- a systematic shift
shared between channels would move both coherently, and the
inverse-variance formula would then understate the true combined
uncertainty.
Per the analyst, this is resolved by how each constraint is obtained
(Sec.~\ref{sec:syst:sources}): every systematic here is derived from a
dedicated control sample reconstructed separately for the electron and muon
channels, rather than from one external number applied to both, so the two
channels' constraints are independent measurements for the same reason
their data statistics are.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
Two apparent discrepancies with the code were raised against an earlier
version of this section and have since been clarified by the analyst
(Sec.~\ref{sec:fit:templates}, Sec.~\ref{sec:fit:modifiers}): the
$e$-channel notebook cell's mismatch with the intended
signal-enriched/generic-MC template split reflects later, unrelated
$e$-channel tests rather than the configuration behind the numbers above;
and the four generic-$B\bar{B}$ categories shown fixed in
Fig.~\ref{fig:pyhf-config} are deliberately left without a normalisation
modifier because their systematic uncertainties are not yet studied, not an
omission.
Neither is treated as changing the numbers above.
The parameter of interest is the $B\to D\tau\nu$ normalisation, not $R(D)$
itself (Sec.~\ref{sec:fit:poi}); the sensitivity above should not yet be
read as ``the expected uncertainty on $R(D)$'' until that relation is
written down.
